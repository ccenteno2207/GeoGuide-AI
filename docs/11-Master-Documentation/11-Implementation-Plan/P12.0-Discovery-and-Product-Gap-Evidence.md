## Authority and discovered objective

The roadmap names P12 **Real Road Pilot**. Canonical product and architecture documents describe a real-road pilot as a future validation of the existing route and POI experience over a controlled route and dataset. They do not define a frozen P12 contract, route, participant protocol, telemetry policy, success thresholds, release channel, or production-readiness exception for the open P11 backup debt.

This is a material product and operational scope decision. No P12 contract or implementation is authorized by this discovery alone.

## Dependencies and risks

P12 would depend on the existing mobile/backend/routing/data stack, HTTPS, physical-device access, route/dataset selection, permissions policy, privacy/consent, evidence capture and rollback. P11-BACKUP-01 blocks Production Readiness but does not automatically block development discovery or a controlled non-production pilot. It must not be silently treated as PASS.

A real-road pilot could introduce sensitive location, participant, audio or telemetry data. Any continuous tracking, new permission, external service, paid capability, data retention or public exposure requires a separate human decision.

## Required P12 scope decision

Before a P12 contract can be frozen, the project owner must define at least:

1. Whether P12 is a bounded non-production road validation of existing route/POI/Driving flows, or includes new product capabilities.
2. Exact route(s), device(s), participant/test protocol and success thresholds.
3. Whether continuous GPS, tracking, turn-by-turn, ETA, rerouting, proximity alerts, voice or CEFI are included; default remains OUT_OF_SCOPE.
4. Data/telemetry collection, retention, consent and deletion rules.
5. Whether P11 production-readiness debt is an entry blocker for the chosen pilot.
6. Required physical-device and network conditions.
